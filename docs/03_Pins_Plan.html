1. Rules first

Protect the chip All FPGA pins are 3.3 V. Never connect 5 V signals directly (use a level shifter, or a resistor divider for inputs). Keep the current small (an LED with a 330 Ω resistor is fine; motors and relays need a transistor or a driver board). Switch the board off before you change wires.
Names Bitmain called the four signal lines of each connector PLUG, TXD, RXD and RST. For the FPGA they are ordinary I/O pins: you decide in your design if each one is an input or an output. In Ardzy they are called jN[0] to jN[3] (for example j3[1] = connector J3, pin 11).

The I2C pins (3 = SDA, 4 = SCL) of J1..J8 are one shared bus (FPGA pins W19/W18); J9 has its own bus (P18/N17). Both have 1 kΩ pull-ups on the board. Pin 16 gives 3.3 V for small sensors.

LEDs are active low The LEDs are wired from 3.3 V through 220 Ω to the FPGA pin, so a 0 lights them. Write assign leds = ~pattern; in Verilog, or ~pattern & 0xF in Python.

The fan speed pins are normal 3.3 V inputs with 10 kΩ pull-ups, also usable for other signals (the FM transmitter project used them for I2S audio input). The board-ID pins are fixed straps: read them, never drive them.

7. Using the pins

  1. In Vivado, add projects\ardzy_pins.xdc to your constraints (it is also in every new FPGA project made with the Ardzy app).
  2. Remove the # in front of the lines you use, and give your top-level ports the same names (for example output [3:0] j4).
  3. In the Ardzy app, the Pins tab shows all of this; click a row to copy its Vivado line.
# example: J1 as a UART
set_property -dict {PACKAGE_PIN T12 IOSTANDARD LVCMOS33} [get_ports {j1[1]}] ;# TX out, J1 pin 11
set_property -dict {PACKAGE_PIN U12 IOSTANDARD LVCMOS33} [get_ports {j1[2]}] ;# RX in,  J1 pin 12
