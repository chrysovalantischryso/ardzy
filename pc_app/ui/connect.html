How to connect things to Ardzy

The 9 connectors J1 to J9, the console header, and how to wire LEDs, buttons, serial devices (UART), sensors (I2C) and motors. Every wiring here belongs to an example: Examples (top bar) shows the same wiring next to each one.

Rules first (read this once)

The pins are 3.3 V and have no protection. Never connect 5 V (or more) to a pin: not from an Arduino Uno, not from a 5 V sensor, not from a USB-UART adapter set to 5 V. It can destroy the FPGA. A 5 V device needs a level shifter or, for a signal going into Ardzy, a voltage divider (1 kOhm and 2 kOhm, see Arduino Uno).
  • GND together. Every device you connect shares GND with the board (a GND pin of the connector). Without it, nothing works.
  • Small currents only. A pin gives a few mA: enough for an LED with a resistor, a signal, a driver's input. Never a motor, a relay coil or a buzzer directly: use a driver (transistor, ULN2003, motor driver module).
  • Power for motors from their own supply, with only GND shared with Ardzy.
  • Wire with the power off, check twice, then switch on.
  • The board itself runs from its 12 V input or 5 V. Do not power it from 3.3 V (its regulators need more).

The connectors J1 to J9

The 9 connectors (2 rows of 9 pins, 2.54 mm) were the hash-board plugs of the miner. All 9 have the same pins. The pin numbers are printed next to each connector.

Drawn like the app's Map view (Map, Board, click a connector): pin 1 is the square pad. In your code, J_5 is J1_5 on J1, J2_5 on J2 and so on.

That makes 36 I/O pins (4 on each of 9 connectors). The board also has its own: 4 small FPGA LEDs (LED0 ... LED3), the green and red status LEDs, the IP and Reset buttons (BUTTON_IP), a buzzer, and 6 fan headers. The Map view of the app shows where everything is on the board.

UART (serial) devices

The board has two kinds of UART. Do not mix them up:

TX always goes to RX: the board's TX to the device's RX, the device's TX to the board's RX. And GND to GND.

A USB-UART adapter (CP2102, CH340, FT232) (examples: UsbUartAdapter, or the app's cable link)

  for the app's cable link (console):        for your sketch (Serial1):
  adapter TX  --->  J12 pin 1 (RX)           adapter TX  --->  J1 pin 12 (RX)
  adapter GND ----  J12 pin 2 (GND)          adapter GND ----  J1 pin 9  (GND)
  adapter RX  <---  J12 pin 3 (TX)           adapter RX  <---  J1 pin 11 (TX)
  adapter 5V / 3V3: not connected            adapter 5V / 3V3: not connected

If the adapter has a 5V / 3V3 jumper, set it to 3V3. No text at all: swap the TX and RX wires (it does no harm).

The first test: one wire (example: UartLoopback)

  J1 pin 11 (TX)  ---->  J1 pin 12 (RX)          what is sent comes straight back

Or a 4-channel level shifter module (LV side to Ardzy 3.3 V, HV side to the Arduino's 5 V).

An ESP32, ESP8266, Raspberry Pi Pico (3.3 V) (example: ESP32Link)

  ESP32 RX2 (GPIO16) <---  J1 pin 11 (TX)
  ESP32 TX2 (GPIO17) --->  J1 pin 12 (RX)
  ESP32 GND          ----  J1 pin 9  (GND)        both 3.3 V: no resistors

A GPS module (NEO-6M, NEO-8M, BN-220) (example: GPSReader)

  GPS VCC  ----  J1 pin 16 (3.3 V)
  GPS GND  ----  J1 pin 9  (GND)
  GPS TX   --->  J1 pin 12 (RX)          9600 baud on most modules
  GPS RX   <---  J1 pin 11 (TX)          (only to change its settings)

I2C sensors and displays

Four wires: power, GND, SDA, SCL. Many devices can share the same two I2C wires, each has its own address. Run the I2CScanner example first: it lists every device it finds.

  sensor VCC  ----  J1 pin 16 (3.3 V)
  sensor GND  ----  J1 pin 1  (GND)
  sensor SDA  ----  J1 pin 3  (SDA)       the same on J1..J8 (one bus: Wire)
  sensor SCL  ----  J1 pin 4  (SCL)       J9 is a second bus (Wire1)
DeviceAddressExample

BMP280 / BME280 temperature, pressure0x76 (SDO high: 0x77)BMP280

The board already has pull-up resistors on SDA and SCL. A module's own pull-ups are fine too. Use 3.3 V modules (most breakout boards are).

LEDs and buttons (example: ExternalLedButton)

  J2 pin 5  ---[ 330 Ohm ]---|>|--- J2 pin 9 (GND)       LED: long leg (+) to the resistor, short leg to GND
  J2 pin 11 -------[ button ]------ J2 pin 10 (GND)      pressed = LOW (the pin has a pull-up inside)

220 Ohm to 1 kOhm for the LED resistor. For a brighter LED strip, a relay or anything bigger: a transistor or a module in between (the pin only switches it).

Motors

A servo (SG90, MG996R) (example: ServoSweep, Blocks "turn servo")

  servo signal (orange)  <---  J5 pin 5        any I/O pin
  servo + (red)          ----  +5 V of a separate 5 V supply
  servo - (brown)        ----  GND of that supply AND a GND pin of Ardzy

A small stepper 28BYJ-48 with a ULN2003 board (example: StepperULN2003, Blocks "4-wire stepper")

  ULN2003 IN1..IN4  <---  J1 pin 5, 11, 12, 15
  ULN2003 GND       ----  J1 pin 9 and the 5 V supply's GND
  ULN2003 +5V       ----  +5 V of a 5 V supply (a USB charger)

A bigger stepper with a STEP/DIR driver (A4988, DRV8825, TMC2208) (example: StepperDriver, Blocks "stepper with STEP")

  driver STEP  <---  J1 pin 5          driver VDD  ----  J1 pin 16 (3.3 V)
  driver DIR   <---  J1 pin 11         driver GND  ----  J1 pin 9
  driver EN    <---  J1 pin 15         driver VMOT ----  12 V motor supply (+ 100 uF capacitor)

Set the driver's current limit before the first run, and never unplug the motor while the motor supply is on.

Fans

The 6 fan headers of the board take normal 4-pin PC fans (12 V). The examples FanControl and the Blocks "set the fans to" control their speed.

Power

  • The board: its 12 V input (the 6-pin PCIe plug of a PC power supply) or 5 V.
  • Pin 16 (3.3 V out) of every connector: for sensors, GPS, small modules.
  • Motors, LED strips, relays: their own supply. Only GND is shared with the board.
